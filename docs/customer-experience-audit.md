# Ivera Customer Experience Audit
**Date:** 2026-06-09  
**Persona:** International tourist, 28–45, visiting Georgia for the first time, found Ivera from Instagram or word of mouth. Has WhatsApp. Not a gamer. Moderate comfort with apps.

---

## 1. Do I Understand What Ivera Is?

**Answer: Partially — after 30 seconds of reading**

Within the first 3 seconds the tourist sees:
- "Ivera" (unfamiliar word)
- "✦ Georgia Travel Quests ✦" (small text)
- "Your journey begins in Georgia." (generic, could be any travel site)

After 10 seconds (reading sub-headline):
- "Taste 8,000-year-old wine. Walk fortified mountain cities. Complete missions and earn XP on real guided tours."

The phrase "Complete missions and earn XP" signals a game mechanic — but a tourist who doesn't play games may find this confusing. Does "missions" mean they have to do homework? Are these self-guided or with a guide? What is XP in a travel context?

**What makes it click:** The "How it Works" section (Step 3: "Scan QR codes, taste, observe, and discover at each stop") finally explains the concept clearly. But it's below the fold and most tourists won't scroll to it on first visit.

**Verdict:** A motivated tourist will understand within 20 seconds. A casual browser will bounce before understanding.

---

## 2. Do I Know What to Click First?

**Answer: No — there are three competing first actions**

From the hero section alone, a tourist can:
1. Click "Start Your Journey" (gold button) → opens a form asking for name
2. Click "Build Custom Trip on WhatsApp" → opens WhatsApp
3. Click "Explore all 8 routes →" (tiny text) → /tours

There is no obvious single next step. "Start Your Journey" sounds like the primary action, but it leads to a profile creation form — not a tour catalog. A tourist who expects to see tours after clicking that button is immediately confused.

The tour cards visible below the fold provide the clearest "what this is" signal, but only if the tourist scrolls.

**Verdict:** First-time tourist is likely to either tap the WhatsApp button (lowest friction, most familiar action) or bounce. The intended onboarding flow (Start Journey → Explorer Pass) has too much friction before showing value.

---

## 3. Do I Know What Is Free and What Is Paid?

**Answer: No — free vs. paid distinction is absent**

The free Tbilisi mini-quest is not mentioned anywhere on the main navigation or homepage. The tours listing shows prices (55–150 GEL / person) but there is no "Try for Free" option visible.

A tourist reading the homepage believes:
- All experiences are paid
- WhatsApp booking is the only way to start

The "free" angle is completely hidden. This is the most significant missed opportunity — tourists who can try before buying are dramatically more likely to convert.

**The only place free is mentioned:**
- Inside `/free-tbilisi-quest` itself (accessible only via direct URL)
- The "Explorer Pass is free" subtext in the hero section (referring to profile, not the quest)

**Verdict:** Tourist has no idea a free option exists unless told by someone who knows the URL.

---

## 4. Do I Trust the Company?

**Answer: Not yet — needs 3 trust signals that are missing**

**Current trust builders (working):**
- "No payment now" badge — excellent, removes biggest objection
- WhatsApp phone number (995555443787) visible in the message — feels like a real person
- "Levani confirms within 2 hours" — specific and believable
- Tour detail pages are detailed and professional

**Missing trust builders:**
- **No face.** "Levani" is a name in a sentence, not a person. A photo removes skepticism faster than any copy.
- **No testimonials.** A tourist booking a guided tour from a stranger needs social proof. The complete absence of any reviews or quotes creates doubt.
- **No social media presence linked.** No Instagram, no Facebook, no TripAdvisor. For a tour guide, this is unusual and suspicious.
- **No about/contact page.** Where is this company based? Is it registered? Can I email them?

**Verdict:** A tourist with high intent (already decided to visit Georgia) will trust enough to message on WhatsApp. A tourist in discovery mode will not convert without at least one of: photo, testimonials, or social proof.

---

## 5. Do I Understand What Happens During a Quest?

**Answer: Somewhat — but it requires reading multiple pages**

The best explanation of "what happens during a quest" is the tour detail page, which shows:
- Mission list with location, description, and type (QR Scan / Observation / Taste)
- "Unlocks on the tour" label
- The "How it works" section on homepage (Step 3)

What a tourist might still misunderstand:
- **QR scanning is the primary mechanic** — this isn't stated upfront. "Scan QR codes" appears in the homepage "How it Works" section but isn't reinforced on tour detail pages.
- **The guide is present during the whole tour** — this is implied but not stated. Does the guide lead them to the QR codes or do they wander alone?
- **The quest is during the tour, not a separate activity** — the word "quest" could imply a separate day trip

The free Tbilisi mini-quest actually provides the best demonstration — but nobody finds it.

**Verdict:** A tourist who reads the tour detail page mission section will understand roughly what to expect. But the QR mechanic and guide role could be explained more explicitly.

---

## 6. Do I Understand How Booking Works?

**Answer: Yes — this is the clearest part of the product**

The booking widget is intuitive:
1. Pick a date
2. Pick number of people
3. See price calculation update in real time
4. See discount (if any) appear automatically
5. Click "Book via WhatsApp"
6. WhatsApp opens with professional pre-filled message

The "No payment now" badge appears near the price. The message includes the booking code. The process is clear.

**Minor confusions:**
- After clicking "Book via WhatsApp" and WhatsApp opens, the app shows "Booking sent!" — but the booking isn't actually confirmed. Levani hasn't replied yet. The "sent!" state feels like a confirmation when it's actually just a WhatsApp message.
- First-time WhatsApp users might not know how to complete the send in WhatsApp.
- There's no "what happens next" explanation after the button is pressed.

**Verdict:** Best UX in the product. Small improvement opportunity around post-booking expectations.

---

## 7. Do I Feel Motivated to Create Explorer Pass?

**Answer: No — value proposition is missing**

The "Start Your Journey" CTA opens a form asking for:
- Name (required)
- Country (optional)
- Interests (chips: Wine / Mountains / History / Food / Adventure)

Nothing before this form tells the tourist what they will get from creating a profile. The modal appears and asks for information without offering anything in return from the user's perspective.

A tourist who dismisses the modal has no sense of loss. They didn't know they were going to get XP tracking, personalized recommendations, or a badge collection.

**The motivation to create a pass only appears AFTER creating one** — when the dashboard shows XP progress and an earned badge (if they've already done something). It's circular: you need the pass to see the value, but you need to see the value to want the pass.

**Verdict:** Add one sentence of value proposition before the form. "Track your XP, unlock badges, and get personalised tour picks across all of Georgia." That's enough to tip most people into filling it out.

---

## 8. Do I Feel Motivated to Share My Results?

**Answer: Somewhat — Hero Chronicle is good, but sharing is limited**

After completing a paid quest:
- The "Generate My Hero Chronicle" button appears
- Gemini AI writes a cinematic story of their adventure
- A "Send Chronicle via WhatsApp" button appears

The chronicle mechanic is genuinely differentiated and emotionally resonant. The problem is the only sharing option is WhatsApp — a private channel. The story goes to Levani (as feedback/marketing) but there's no mechanism to share it with friends, post it on Instagram, or copy it to a message to send home.

The badge system also has no sharing. A "Key of Tbilisi" badge on a profile page that nobody else can see has limited motivational power.

**Missing sharing moments:**
- Copy chronicle to clipboard
- Download badge as image
- Share completed quest on Instagram Stories
- Show friends your profile (public URL doesn't exist)

**Verdict:** The emotional hooks are built (chronicle, badge, XP). The sharing infrastructure is missing.

---

## 9. Would I Book a Tour From This Site?

**As a high-intent tourist (already decided on Georgia):** Yes, probably — the pricing is clear, "no payment now" eliminates risk, and WhatsApp is familiar.

**As a discovery-mode tourist (browsing options):** No — because:
1. No social proof (why trust this over booking.com or a hotel tour desk?)
2. No photo of the guide (who is Levani?)
3. No answer to "what if something goes wrong?"
4. No alternative contact method if WhatsApp fails

**Verdict: 6/10** — converts motivated buyers, loses browsers.

---

## 10. What Would Stop Me?

**Ranked by likelihood of causing drop-off:**

1. **"Who is Levani?"** — No face, no bio, no story. Private WhatsApp booking with a stranger is a high-trust ask. A photo resolves this immediately.

2. **"This might not be real."** — No reviews, no TripAdvisor rating, no social media links. The professional design is present but unsupported.

3. **"I don't understand what I'm getting."** — The "quest" concept needs one more sentence of plain-language explanation before the tourist is asked to act.

4. **"I don't know what to do first."** — Three CTAs with different purposes compete for attention. One clear path would convert better.

5. **"I don't know if this is cheaper than a regular tour."** — No price comparison, no "what makes this better than [competitor]" framing.

6. **"The Explorer Pass form appeared out of nowhere."** — The form has no explained value. Easy to dismiss.

7. **"What if I need to cancel?"** — No cancellation policy visible anywhere. This is a concern for international tourists with changing itineraries.

## Quick Wins (Changes That Cost < 1 Day Each)

1. **Add "Try Free Quest" link to homepage** — one anchor link in the hero section
2. **Add Levani photo** — one image + two sentences, any page
3. **Add value sentence to Explorer Pass modal** — one line of copy above the form
4. **Add "What happens next?" note after booking** — one paragraph below the success state
5. **Add FAQ accordion** on tour detail pages — 5 questions, 20 minutes to write
6. **Reduce hero gradient opacity** — let the Kazbegi photo actually breathe

## Bigger Improvements (1–3 Days Each)

1. **Testimonials section** — even 3 real quotes from beta testers transforms the trust level
2. **Levani about section** — photo + bio + number of tours + languages spoken
3. **Social sharing for Hero Chronicle** — "Copy text" + "Download as image"
4. **Fix leaderboard to show real data** or clearly label it as "Demo / Coming Soon"
5. **Free quest entry points** — homepage banner, tours page card, nav tooltip
